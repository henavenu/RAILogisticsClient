Today's demo answers one question: when an AI agent acts in more than one country, who decides how much it is allowed to do on its own? Our answer is the agent passport. The same Cross-Border Logistics agent, doing the same release, gets full autonomy in the US and is held for a Qualified Person in the EU. The Trust platform enforces that at runtime. What we are showcasing

Authority has to travel with the agent

The problem

  • Agents now act across jurisdictions under one identity.
  • What one country authorises, another may not.
  • Model behaviour alone cannot hold that line.

The passport

  • A portable record per agent: owner, risk tier, validity.
  • One stamp per jurisdiction, each with its own agency and oversight model.
  • Sovereignty, lineage of delegated authority, revocation.

The enforcement

  • The passport is stamped onto every governed action.
  • The Trust platform reads the stamp and applies the oversight it requires.
  • Every hold and every certification lands in the audit trail.
02
Speaker notes

Three ideas. First, agents increasingly act across borders under a single identity, and what the US authorises is not what the EU authorises. Second, the passport records that difference explicitly: who owns the agent, its risk tier, and a stamp per jurisdiction saying what it may do there and how a human oversees it. Third, the passport is not a document on a shelf. It is stamped onto each governed action, and the Trust platform enforces the oversight that stamp requires, with a full audit trail.

The client

PharmaLogistics: a 13-agent pharma distribution fleet

What the agents do

  • Order Intake routes every request to a specialist.
  • Inventory Agent and Facility Desk read positions and DEA licence standing.
  • Route Optimizer places domestic replenishment orders.
  • Cross-Border Logistics releases cold-chain consignments US → EU.
  • Diversion Review Board: four reviewers and a coordinator decide held controlled-substance orders.

How it is governed

  • A standalone app. It reaches the Trust platform only through its APIs, with an app credential.
  • The RAI Trust SDK discovers and registers all agents and evaluates every tool call and agent-to-agent handoff.
  • Seven policy packs, including sanctions, agent coordination, EU AI Act and the new agent_passport pack.
  • Passport enforcement is configuration on the platform: a rule, a pack and a policy. No platform code changed.

04
Speaker notes

Tiers come straight from our primer, not a vendor score. Three dimensions (agency, impact and reach) are each scored one to four, and the highest sets the tier. Two amplifiers each add a level: coordinating with other agents, and acting outside a boundary you control. Swarm behaviour or a cross-jurisdiction boundary set Tier 4 on their own. That is why Cross-Border Logistics is Tier 4 even though it only executes. Most of the fleet is Tier 3 because the agents coordinate or touch regulated data. Higher tier buys a shorter leash: a Tier 4 passport is valid for six months.

Human oversight models

Oversight follows agency, and is stamped per jurisdiction

HITLHuman in the loop · high oversight

Every consequential action is approved by a human before it happens.

For Instruct and Assist agency

In the demo: EU release. A Qualified Person certifies each batch before release. Also the Diversion Review Board escalation.
HOTLHuman on the loop · medium oversight

The agent acts; a human monitors and can intervene.

For Execute agency

In the demo: US export release and domestic replenishment. The agent acts, the platform evaluates every action, the QA officer watches the trail.
HICHuman in command · low oversight

Oversight at the system level, per mandate rather than per decision.

For Delegate agency

In the demo: no agent holds Delegate today. The board's delegation to the deployment owner is the mandate an HIC agent would run under.
05
Speaker notes

The primer ties oversight to agency. Instruct and Assist mean a human in the loop: nothing consequential happens without approval. Execute means a human on the loop: the agent acts, and a human monitors and can step in. Delegate means a human in command: oversight is set at the level of the mandate, not each decision. The key move in this demo is that oversight is stamped per jurisdiction. The same agent is on the loop in the US and in the loop in the EU, and the platform knows which applies at the moment of action.

Speaker notes

This is what is live today: one corporate group, two jurisdictions. Each stamp records the local legal entity, the applicable law, what the agent may do there and who granted it. In the US the QA release officer granted Execute; in the EU the Qualified Person granted only Assist, because EU law requires a QP to certify each batch. The agent's capability ceiling is Execute, so there is a gap between what it can do and what the EU allows. That gap is closed by policy on the platform, not by hoping the model behaves, and the passport makes the gap visible to an auditor.

Agent passport · Cross-organisational agreement NEXT · THIRD-PARTY AGENT

When the counterparty is another company's agent

Cross-jurisdiction (today)

  • One owner organisation; subsidiaries per country.
  • Stamps issued by our own legal entities.
  • Trust comes from our internal delegation chain.
  • Trust boundary: cross-jurisdiction → T4.

Cross-organisation (adding next)

  • A third-party carrier or importer runs its own agent with its own passport.
  • Each passport is issued by its controlling organisation; trust comes from verifying the other side's passport, not from a sovereign.
  • At handoff, our agent checks the counterpart's passport: issuer, tier, stamp for this jurisdiction, validity, revocation status.
  • The agreement between the organisations sets scope, data sharing, liability and who can revoke.
  • Reach becomes cross-organizational → T4 on reach as well.
07
Speaker notes

Today's demo is cross-jurisdictional within one group. The next step, which we are adding tomorrow, is cross-organisational: a third-party carrier's agent with its own passport. The model changes in one important way. A passport is issued by the organisation that controls the agent, so trust between companies comes from verifying each other's passports at the handoff, against an agreement that defines scope, data sharing, liability and revocation. Our agent would refuse, or route to a human, if the counterpart's passport has no valid stamp for the jurisdiction or has been revoked.

Runtime enforcement

How an EU release is held, certified and released

  1. Agent proposesCross-Border Logistics prepares a release to Germany.app
  2. Passport stampedEU · assist · HITL · T4 is attached to the call.app · passports.py
  3. Platform holdsThe passport_oversight rule scores NORMATIVE; the policy's HITL trigger requires human review.Trust platform
  4. QP decidesThe item appears in Human Review; the Qualified Person approves.Trust dashboard
  5. Release resumesRe-stamped QP-CERTIFIED with the reviewer's name; passes.app → platform
  6. AuditThe hold and the certified release sit side by side in the Eval Trail.Trust dashboard

A US release takes steps 1, 2 and 5 directly: the stamp says HOTL, so the platform lets it through and monitors.

08
Speaker notes

Here is the mechanism. The agent proposes the release. Before the call is governed, the app stamps the passport for the destination onto it. The Trust platform has a rule that reads the stamp: a HITL stamp scores NORMATIVE, and an app-scoped policy with a HITL trigger turns that into a required human review, so the SDK pauses the agent. The Qualified Person approves in Human Review. When the release resumes, the stamp records the certification and who gave it, so the audit trail shows which authority the human exercised. For a US release the stamp says HOTL and the action simply proceeds under monitoring.
